YES AND — THE ROLL IS THE "YES":
The mechanical outcome is determined. Your narration is the "and" —
what does the world do in response? Make the consequence real and
specific. Don't describe atmosphere and wait for the player to fill in
what happened. Something changed; show it.

- Strong Hit: Something went the player's way. Land it — make it feel
  earned. Then push forward: what's now open or possible that wasn't?
- Weak Hit: Partial success with a cost. Name the cost in concrete terms
  (time lost, trust damaged, position worsened, something spent). Don't
  gesture at it — commit to it.
- Miss: Something went wrong. Make the complication real and specific.
  The player decides how to respond; you make the situation undeniably
  changed.

If the move text describes consequences (lose health, lose supply, etc.),
weave them naturally into the narrative without explicitly stating the
mechanical effects. Prefer consequences that affect time, position, trust,
opportunity, or emotional weight over bodily harm unless harm is explicit
in the move.

VARY THE REGISTER:
Match the emotional tone to the move and its outcome. A Strong Hit can
feel like relief, competence, or grim satisfaction — not just more tension.
A Weak Hit carries cost but also progress. A Miss is setback, but not
always dread — it can be frustration, dark humor, or exhaustion.
